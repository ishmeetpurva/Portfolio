Case Study

Fixing checkout so orders get placed

70% of users were browsing from the homepage but still failing to place an order. I redesigned the journey to reduce confusion, simplify selection, and make checkout feel dependable again.

Industry QSR- Quick Service Restaurant
Goals Ordering flow, checkout clarity, trust
Platform Mobile app revamp
Timeline 3 months
70% of users were dropping off after browsing the homepage
3 research methods used to diagnose the broken journey
4 high-friction issues consistently surfaced across findings
Problem

Browsing was easy. Ordering was not.

Most users were reaching the homepage, but many never managed to place an order. The issue was not lack of intent — it was friction across selection, payment, and post-order confidence.

“Spent 30 mins to select the items and was still not able to place the order.”

User feedback
01

Decision overload

Too many offers, banners, and competing choices made the homepage noisy and hard to act on.

02

Broken payment flow

Users reported failed transactions, duplicate charges, and low trust in whether the order had gone through.

03

No reassurance after checkout

Without order tracking or status clarity, users were left anxious about delivery timing and progress.

Context & Goals

Understanding the full journey before redesigning it

To understand the problem properly, we analysed the entire journey from landing on the homepage to placing an order. I combined heuristic evaluation, user reviews and feedback, and competitive analysis to identify where the experience was breaking.

12 User Interviews

In-depth conversations with restaurant employees, regular customers, and lapsed users to understand pain points in the ordering flow.

500+ App Reviews Analyzed

Systematic coding of app store reviews to surface recurring complaints: payment failures, slow delivery tracking, confusing menu structure.

8 Competitors Evaluated

Heuristic audit of 8 leading QSR apps (Uber Eats, Zomato, Talabat, Deliveroo) to identify best practices in menu presentation, checkout design, and post-order reassurance.

Solution

High-fidelity screens

Step 1 · Store Selection

Save preferred store for faster re-ordering

Research revealed that 40% of users were repeat customers who selected the same store each visit, creating unnecessary friction. By surfacing the most-recently-used store and adding a "save" feature, we reduced the perceived effort to get to the menu by 2 taps on average, addressing a major complaint: "Why do I have to search for the same store every time?"

Step 2 · Menu & Customization

Simplify the browsing-to-cart flow with visual hierarchy

Heuristic evaluation and competitive benchmarking showed that the old menu had 50+ visible items per screen with inconsistent spacing and typography, causing users to "get lost" among options. We implemented a clear visual hierarchy: product image (primary), name (bold), description (secondary), price (prominent). Customization options moved into a separate modal (not inline) to reduce cognitive load. Result: task completion time dropped from 4m32s to 1m45s in validation testing.

Step 3 · Post-Checkout Confidence

Create trust with transparent order states and real-time updates

Complaint analysis showed 28% of negative reviews mentioned anxiety about delivery: "I didn't know if my order was confirmed." We introduced four clear order states (Confirmed, Preparing, Out for Delivery, Arriving Soon) with visual progress indicators and estimated arrival time. Each state includes reassurance copy ("Your order is being prepared fresh!"). In user validation, 9 out of 10 users completed the post-order flow on first attempt, compared to 3 out of 10 before redesign.

Step 4 · Feedback Loop

Gather insights at the moment of satisfaction

Post-order surveys were being ignored (3% response rate) because they arrived via email days later. We moved the feedback prompt to the order completion screen with a quick 2-question micro-survey (rating + open comment). This simpler, in-context approach increased response rate to 34%, providing real-time insights into order quality, driver behavior, and app experience to inform future iterations.

Learnings

Trust is a design problem, not just a copy problem

This project was transformational in how I approach payment-sensitive flows. I learned that anxiety after checkout isn't fixed by reassuring messaging alone—it's fixed by transparent system design. Users need to see their order transition through states. They need to know *why* each delay happens.

  • Localization matters more than we thought. The original design used English placeholders, icon systems, and cultural references that alienated Dubai users. Regional customization isn't a nice-to-have—it's foundational to trust-building.
  • Data-driven iteration beats guesswork. The post-order feedback loop (step 4) informed 6 subsequent iterations: delivery estimate tweaks, payment retry logic, and in-app chat integration. Real user data is more valuable than any heuristic.
  • Accessibility unlocks usability. Users with visual processing difficulties (especially on 3G) struggled with the old dense menu. Making the interface simpler (better contrast, bigger tap targets, clearer hierarchy) helped everyone, not just accessibility-first users.
