Case Study

Redesigning an overwhelming homepage so users know what to do next

CompanyGetSociable
RoleUX Designer (sole designer)
Timeline6 months (May–Oct 2024)
Research MethodsUser Interviews, Surveys, Hotjar
GetSociable is an early-stage events app for young professionals. The founding team needed engagement and retention numbers for their next funding round.Read more

That meant fixing the product experience before scaling acquisition — the app had to hold the users it already had. This project covered navigation, the homepage, and group planning.

Problem

A cluttered app, no planning tools, and users dropping off before they could start.

01

Navigation was unclear

Read more

Users couldn't tell what to do next. Filters, sorting and location switching were missing or buried.

02

The homepage was overwhelming

Read more

Every section carried equal weight, so the primary action never stood out. Redundant sections and small text added to the load.

03

No way to plan or book ahead

Read more

Group outings were organised outside the app, in WhatsApp threads. Nothing in the product let users coordinate or secure a plan.

Context & Goals

Used three methods to research user pain points

Each method is paired with the one insight that shaped the design.

01 · 150+ Survey Responses

In-app survey across all user segments.

Key insight

Navigation was the top complaint across every segment — by a significant margin.

→ Set the navigation & discovery fixes

02 · Hotjar Session Recordings

Session recordings and heatmaps of new-user homepage visits.

Key insight

Users spent 2–3 minutes on the homepage, then left without a single tap on a core feature.

→ Set the homepage brief

03 · 15+ User Interviews

Face-to-face sessions with active users, churned users, and group organisers.

Key insight

Users weren't asking for a trip planner — they were describing the exact problem one would solve.

→ Became the Trip Planner

"We arrived and they said they couldn't fit us in — we had to wait half an hour. If we had booked in advance… but we didn't really know how busy it would be."

Charlotte, user interview — on not planning group outings in advance
Process

From insight to wireframe

Before moving to high-fidelity, I sketched the key flows to validate the structure and interactions early.

The flow this replaced

Planning a group outing used to leave the app entirely.

Before · outside the app
Find an event in the app→Screenshot it→Paste into WhatsApp→Long group thread→Walk in & hope for a table
After · one in-app flow
Find an event→Create a plan→Invite the group→Vote on options→Book the table

I explored two alternatives first — event-sharing links and in-app group chat — and discarded both: sharing didn't solve coordination, and chat would have competed with WhatsApp instead of replacing it.

Solution

Strategic shifts that improved user retention by 3%

Three shifts — one for each problem, each shown before and after.

Solves problem 01 · Navigation

Small win — Navigation & discovery fixes

Quick filters, a location switcher in the search bar, save-from-anywhere, sorting, and a full filter panel — the control users had been asking for.

Solves problem 02 · Homepage

Meaningful shift — Homepage redesign

Restructured the hierarchy so booking a trip is visually dominant, and cut the redundant sections.

Solves problem 03 · Planning

Big bet — Trip Planner

A collaborative planning feature. Nearly every user described the problem it solves, and it gave GetSociable an answer to "why not just use WhatsApp?"

Learnings

A new feature can reframe an entire product

The Trip Planner gave GetSociable a clear answer to "why use this instead of WhatsApp?" — which made every other design decision feel more intentional.
